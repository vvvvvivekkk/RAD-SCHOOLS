# Grade 3 — Academic-Year Curriculum (CT+AI Lab · 36 weeks × 60 min)

**Branch:** `curriculum/grade-3-year-v1` · **Base:** `curriculum/mapping-matrix-v1` + `cbse-ctai-archive/framework/grade-3/`
**Delivered in:** separate "CT+AI Lab" period, not stolen from Math (per Grade-3 reality-check decision)
**Infrastructure assumption:** Tier-2/3 school · 40+ kids · one projector · shared devices · unreliable Wi-Fi · bilingual classroom (Telugu / Urdu / English)

---

## 1. Grade-at-a-glance

A Grade 3 student (age 8) walks out of this year able to: name five places AI already lives in their home (UPI autocomplete, YouTube Shorts auto-caption, Google Maps, voice assistant, auto-focus on a phone camera); point to six parts inside a computer and say what each does; follow an unplugged algorithm; recognise a pattern and extend it using kolam / rangoli grammar; encode and decode a message in English and in Telugu or Urdu using a Caesar wheel they made themselves; build a four-scene ScratchJr story on a shared tablet; make a paper-track line-following robot move around a drawing they made; and finish the year with a hand-drawn "AI in my village" poster + 3-sentence caption, displayed at the annual parent-day showcase. Zero screen-time dependency: 23 of 36 sessions run on paper, cardboard, scissors, and chalk. The three device-days per term (ScratchJr tablet, voice-assistant demo, line-follower) are the parent-visible moments — every one has an offline fallback if Wi-Fi dies.

  - *Signature 2 (Week 15):* Kolam pattern-of-the-week.

## 4. Year-end capstone brief

**Title:** *AI in My Village* — hand-drawn A3 poster + 3-sentence trilingual caption.

**Duration:** 6 weeks (Weeks 30-35), ~5 hours of classroom time + ~2 hours homework (home observation).

**Deliverable:** one A3 poster per student showing 4-6 places AI lives in their family's daily life (UPI payment, Google Maps directions, YouTube captions, voice assistant, auto-dial on a phone, auto-brightness on a TV, washing-machine fuzzy-logic, Jio/Airtel call-recording transcript). Each place labelled with (a) its name, (b) one sentence in Telugu or Urdu, (c) one sentence in English. Poster is signed by the student and mounted on the school wall for Week 36 annual showcase.

**Why this capstone:** closes the three-year "AI around me" thread opened at Week 1, gives every parent a visual artefact on the wall at pickup for Week 36, and sidesteps every infrastructure risk — the capstone needs paper, markers, and a parent willing to talk for 15 minutes at home.

**Assessment:** 4×4 rubric (Technical observation accuracy / Ethics — did they ask permission to interview / Communication / Effort) × Novice / Developing / Proficient / Mastery. Portfolio entry: scanned photo of the poster + the home-interview worksheet + the teacher's observation note.

## 5. Week-by-week session plans

### Week 1 — Welcome to CT+AI Lab · "Where Is AI?"

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #1 AI around me — **Introduce**
**CBSE handbook link:** none (CBSE Grade 3 SH never names AI in student-facing pages; the Preface and Introduction on SH pp. 2, 5 do, but no student chapter)
**CBSE placement vs ours:** not in CBSE at G3; CBSE first teaches AI at G6 (`CTAI_Pri6SH_2026-27.pdf` Ch 1 p. 48)
**Danger-zone defense:** none this session
**Sibling-subject collision check:** none (CBSE Coding Handbook starts G6 — no G3 overlap)
**International framework map:** AI4K12 Big Idea 5 Societal Impact at K-2 (`ai/international/ai4k12/`); UNESCO Understand L1 "Foundations of AI knowledge" — recognition tier

**Learning outcomes** (verbs only, assessable)
- Name three places AI shows up at home
- Distinguish "a computer did this" from "a person did this" in five scenarios
- Draw one example in their notebook

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Hardware: nothing. ₹0
- Software: nothing. ₹0
- Printables: 1-page "where is AI?" worksheet (grid of 6 pictures) × 40 = ₹60. Teacher brings own phone for demo.
- Total this session: **₹60**

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher plays a 30-second clip (phone speaker is enough — no projector needed): a voice assistant answering a Telugu question ("నేను ఎలా ఉన్నాను?") and failing, then succeeding. Students laugh. Teacher asks: *"Who heard your voice? Was it a person?"*
- **Acquire (15 min):** Teacher script, one paragraph: *"Some things in your home listen, look, or guess what you want. YouTube knows which video to play next. Google Maps knows the fastest road. Your phone unlocks when it sees your face. These are not people — these are programs that were taught by people. This kind of program is called AI, which stands for Artificial Intelligence. We will spend this whole year learning how it works and where it lives."*
- **Apply (30 min):** Students get the worksheet. Six pictures: a boy using UPI; a girl on Google Maps; an old man opening WhatsApp; a woman at an ATM; a cat; a textbook. Students circle which three involve AI. Pair discussion, then whole-class share. Teacher draws a big "AI / Not AI" T-chart on the board and fills it with their answers.
- **Reflect (5 min):** *"Tell the person next to you one place AI lives in your house. Tomorrow, find one more and tell your mother about it."*

**Offline fallback:** needed none — projector optional. If teacher has no phone, use a printed cartoon of Alexa / Siri.
**Bilingual delivery note:** Teacher conducts Inspire + Reflect in the medium of instruction (Telugu-medium → Telugu; Urdu-medium → Urdu; English-medium → English). Acquire + worksheet labels are bilingual. Glossary — Telugu: AI = కృత్రిమ మేధ (kṛtrima mēdha), computer = కంప్యూటర్; Urdu: AI = مصنوعی ذہانت (masnu'i zehanat), computer = کمپیوٹر; English: Artificial Intelligence, computer.
**Parent-facing output:** not this week.
**Misconception map:**
  1. "AI is a robot." Teacher catch: *"Have you seen a robot at home today? No. But did your mother use UPI? That is AI, and it has no body."*
  2. "The internet is AI." Teacher catch: *"The internet is the road. AI is the car. We'll build one car ourselves later this year."*
**Assessment artefact:** worksheet goes into the student's portfolio folder (new folder per student, labelled).
**Differentiation:** fast learner — add a 6th example of their own invention on the worksheet margin. Struggling — teacher sits with them in Apply and does the first two circles together.

### Week 2 — "AI Scavenger Hunt" (homework-driven)

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #1 AI around me — **Introduce**
**CBSE handbook link:** none
**CBSE placement vs ours:** not in CBSE at G3
**Danger-zone defense:** none
**Sibling-subject collision check:** none
**International framework map:** AI4K12 Big Idea 5 K-2; UNESCO Understand L1

**Learning outcomes**
- Identify three AI uses in their own home (from the homework)
- Report one finding verbally to a partner
- Distinguish "AI listens" vs "AI looks" vs "AI reads"

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Hardware: nothing. ₹0
- Software: nothing. ₹0
- Printables: homework sheet "What AI did my family use yesterday?" × 40 = ₹60
- Total: **₹60**

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher asks: *"Who found AI at home yesterday?"* Hands shoot up. Teacher does a quick-fire round: 20 students, one AI place each.
- **Acquire (15 min):** Teacher introduces three categories on the board — AI that listens (voice assistant, phone transcription), AI that looks (face-unlock, Google Lens, photo-filter), AI that reads (YouTube caption, autocomplete, spam filter). Teacher script: *"Every AI has a sense — some have ears, some have eyes, some have reading-glasses. Later this year we will train one ourselves."*
- **Apply (30 min):** Each student stands up and shares their home AI. Teacher writes it under one of the three columns. Students then copy the full three-column list into their notebook.
- **Reflect (5 min):** Pair-share — *"Which column has the most? Why?"*

**Offline fallback:** session is entirely unplugged.
**Bilingual delivery note:** categories are chalked in all three languages on the board. Telugu: వినేది / చూసేది / చదివేది. Urdu: سننے والا / دیکھنے والا / پڑھنے والا. English: listens / looks / reads.
**Parent-facing output:** no (first parent artefact lands Week 4).
**Misconception map:**
  1. "WhatsApp is AI." Catch: *"WhatsApp is an app. The AI inside is autocomplete and sticker suggestion. The app itself is not the AI."*
  2. "Only new phones have AI." Catch: *"My mother's old Nokia had a T9 keyboard — that was also AI. It was just simpler."*
**Assessment artefact:** homework sheet + notebook three-column list.
**Differentiation:** fast — add a fourth column "AI that writes" (autocomplete, grammar check). Struggling — accept drawings instead of words.

### Week 3 — Meet the Computer (Parts Tray)

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #51 Meet the computer parts tray — **Introduce**
**CBSE handbook link:** CG-3 named in SH p. 5 ("Demonstrate understanding of basic concepts of computers and knowledge of hardware and software") but not operationalised anywhere in the Grade 3 Student Handbook. This session closes that gap.
**CBSE placement vs ours:** not in CBSE at G3. CBSE sibling: `ai/cbse/coding-handbooks/classVI_Coding_Teacher_Handbook.pdf` has an intro to hardware at G6 — our G3 session sits 3 grades earlier, scoped to 6 parts not 20.
**Danger-zone defense:** none this session
**Sibling-subject collision check:** CBSE Coding G6 overlaps conceptually but at much greater depth; we scope to naming + touching only.
**International framework map:** AI4K12 Big Idea 4 "Natural Human Interaction" K-2 band (physical interaction with computing devices); UNESCO Understand L1 "AI Techniques" sub-competency at recognition tier.

**Learning outcomes**
- Name 6 parts: RAM, SD card, motor, LED, speaker, battery
- Match each part to the sense it serves (memory / storage / movement / light / sound / power)
- Assemble a labelled diagram sheet

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Hardware: **one tray** with 6 real parts (second-hand RAM stick ₹80, used SD card ₹50, small DC motor ₹30, 3 LEDs with button cells ₹20, mini buzzer ₹40, 9V battery ₹50) — ₹270 one-time, reused all year
- Software: nothing. ₹0
- Printables: labelled-diagram sheet × 40 = ₹60
- Total this session: **₹330** (first-year); **₹60/year** thereafter
- **Per-student marginal annual cost so far: ₹3.30** (tray ÷ 40 students, 5-year amortisation = ₹60/year/tray is what the P&L carries)

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher brings out the tray wrapped in cloth. Peels it back theatrically. *"Inside every computer are these six things. Today you will touch all of them."*
- **Acquire (15 min):** Teacher holds each up and does a one-line role description. Script: *"This is RAM — the computer's short memory, like a whiteboard. This is an SD card — long memory, like a notebook. This is a motor — makes things turn. This is an LED — makes light. This is a speaker — makes sound. This is a battery — the food."*
- **Apply (30 min):** The class is split into 8 groups of 5. Each group gets the tray for 3 minutes, touches every part, then rotates to the next activity (labelled-diagram sheet) while another group gets the tray. Students draw each part and label it with Telugu / Urdu / English words.
- **Reflect (5 min):** *"If you could add one more part to this tray, what would it be?"* Answers go on the board.

**Offline fallback:** session is already offline.
**Bilingual delivery note:** Telugu — RAM = స్వల్పకాలిక జ్ఞాపకం (short-term memory), SD = దీర్ఘకాలిక (long-term), motor = మోటార్, LED = దీపం, speaker = స్పీకర్, battery = బ్యాటరీ. Urdu — RAM = مختصر یاداشت, SD = لمبی یاداشت, motor = موٹر, LED = بلب, speaker = اسپیکر, battery = بیٹری.
**Parent-facing output:** no (first artefact Week 4).
**Misconception map:**
  1. "RAM and SD are the same." Catch: *"The whiteboard loses writing when you rub it. The notebook keeps it. RAM is the whiteboard. SD is the notebook."*
  2. "The battery is the computer." Catch: *"Can I run my phone without a battery if it is plugged in? Yes. So the battery is the food, not the body."*
**Assessment artefact:** labelled-diagram sheet with all 6 parts named in two languages.
**Differentiation:** fast — label all 6 in all 3 languages. Struggling — label 3 in 1 language.

### Week 4 — SIGNATURE 1 · Bilingual Caesar Wheel

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #34 Caesar cipher — **Reinforce** (with an **Extend** overlay: bilingual wheel is new)
**CBSE handbook link:** SH Ch 3 "What's in a Name?" p. 9 — Activity: Caesar Cipher. Teacher Handbook `CTAI_Pri3TH_2026-27.pdf` Ch 3 p. 9, Launch script p. 10.
**CBSE placement vs ours:** same grade (Reinforce) + bilingual overlay (Extend)
**Danger-zone defense:** none at Grade 3 (danger zones hit G5, G6, G7, G9, G10)
**Sibling-subject collision check:** none
**International framework map:** AI4K12 Big Idea 2 "Representation & Reasoning" K-2 (encoding information); UNESCO Understand L1 "AI Techniques" — tokens and codes.

**Learning outcomes**
- Build a two-disc Caesar wheel with English on one face and Telugu or Urdu on the other
- Encode and decode a message with a shift key
- Pass a key and message to a partner and verify the partner decoded correctly

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Hardware: scissors (shared, school supply); toothpick or thin stick × 40 = ₹20
- Software: nothing
- Printables: Caesar wheel template × 40 = ₹80 (double-sided print, English face + Telugu/Urdu face)
- Total: **₹100** (first parent-visible artefact goes home — see below)

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher writes on the board: `KHOOR`. *"If you crack this in 10 minutes you take the message home to your parents."* No "AI" talk. Just the puzzle. Hands shoot up. Someone eventually says *"HELLO"*. Teacher writes +3 under it.
- **Acquire (15 min):** Teacher reads from SH p. 9 verbatim: *"A well-known example of a cipher is the Caesar Cipher, named after the Roman leader Julius Caesar. He used this method to send important messages securely."* Then connects: *"Computers do the same thing every day when your parents make a UPI payment — they hide the PIN so no one else can read it."* (SH p. 9 own framing.)
- **Apply (30 min):** Each student cuts out both circles (follow SH p. 10: *"Cut out both the two circles, and carefully cut out the arrow on the smaller circle"*), puts the toothpick through, and tests. Then: each pair picks a shift number, encodes their own name once in English, once in Telugu or Urdu. Pass the encoded card + shift number to a friend. First-try-correct pairs win a sticker.
- **Reflect (5 min):** *"What if we gave only the card, not the shift number?"* — introduces the idea of a key.

**Offline fallback:** session is already offline. Zero electricity dependency. This is why it is the signature.
**Bilingual delivery note:** wheel template has Devanagari / Telugu letters on one face and English on the other. Teacher conducts hook and reflect in medium of instruction. Glossary — Telugu: cipher = గుప్త సంకేతం, key = తాళం; Urdu: cipher = گُپت علامت, key = چابی.
**Parent-facing output (end-of-month capsule 1):** each student takes home the wheel + an encoded card addressed to a parent. Teacher note (bilingual): *"Your child encoded their name today — ask them what KHOOR means."*
**Misconception map:**
  1. Confusing key with message (gives friend only one). Catch: *"Did you give them both pieces?"*
  2. Wrapping Z → A missed. Catch: *"After Z, go back to A, like a clock."*
**Assessment artefact:** wheel photo + encoded card photo in portfolio.
**Differentiation:** fast — encode a 2-word message. Struggling — pair with a fast peer and encode just the first letter.

### Week 5 — CBSE Ch 4 Toy Joy (3D Views)

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #17 Pattern recognition — **Reinforce**; #48 debugging — **Reinforce** (visual reasoning)
**CBSE handbook link:** SH Ch 4 "Toy Joy" p. 15-17 (top view, number of faces / edges, cubes with hidden dots). TH Ch 4 p. 21 full teacher script.
**CBSE placement vs ours:** same grade (Reinforce) — we deliver the activity CBSE structures as a worksheet
**Danger-zone defense:** none
**Sibling-subject collision check:** none
**International framework map:** AI4K12 Big Idea 1 Perception K-2 ("different views of a 3D object"); UNESCO Understand L1 — representation.

**Learning outcomes**
- Identify the top view of a given 3D solid
- Count faces and edges of a composite object
- Predict a hidden dot from visible dots

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Hardware: 8 sets of 4 cardboard-block solids (empty juice boxes + bottle caps glued together) = teacher makes once, ₹0 cash cost
- Printables: SH p. 15-16 questions printed if school allows, else written on board. 40 × ₹2 = ₹80
- Total: **₹80**

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher holds up a cardboard box above eye level (students see bottom) and below eye level (students see top). *"Why does the same box look different?"*

This rules out options a and c…"* Then explains the three views: top, front, side.
- **Apply (30 min):** Groups of 5 get a cardboard-block solid. Each group draws its top, front, and side on their notebook. Groups rotate solids every 5 minutes (4 rotations). Last 10 min: answer SH Qs 1-4 individually.
- **Reflect (5 min):** *"If a camera on a drone flies over your school, what view does it see?"* (sets up Grade 5+ drone work)

**Offline fallback:** already offline.
**Bilingual delivery note:** view labels on board in all three languages — top: పై నుండి / اوپر سے / top; front: ఎదురుగా / سامنے سے / front; side: పక్క నుండి / کنارے سے / side.

  2. "Rotating the paper changes the view." Catch — SH p. 17 Q8 note: *"You cannot rotate the question or option images."*
**Assessment artefact:** notebook sketches + Q1-4 answers in portfolio.
**Differentiation:** fast — do Q5-10 as extension. Struggling — stick to Q1-4 with teacher's cardboard solid in front of them.

### Week 7 — CBSE Ch 6 Vacation with My Nani Maa (Day-of-the-Week Logic)

**Rosetta Stone row(s):** #48 debugging — **Reinforce** (step-by-step reasoning); #41 unplugged algorithms — **Reinforce**
**CBSE handbook link:** SH Ch 6 "Vacation with My Nani Maa" p. 21-22. TH Ch 6 p. 32.
**CBSE placement vs ours:** same grade (Reinforce)
**Danger-zone defense:** none
**Sibling-subject collision check:** none
**International framework map:** UNESCO Understand L1 "Applications of AI" — temporal reasoning.

**Learning outcomes**
- Reason about before/after relationships on a day/week scale
- Follow a multi-clue logic puzzle (SH p. 14 "Raj, Aryan, Rahul movies" style)
- Represent the reasoning as a simple table

**Materials & cost per class (40 students)**
- Printables: SH p. 21-22 × 40 = ₹60
- Total: **₹60**

**Session flow (60 min)**
- **Inspire (10 min):** Teacher reads the Raj-Aryan-Rahul clue from SH p. 14: *"Raj did not go on Monday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Aryan went on Friday, Rahul went on Sunday. Which day could Raj have gone?"* Discuss as a class.
- **Acquire (15 min):** Teacher introduces the grid-table method — draw a 3×7 grid, cross out impossible cells, read off what's left. Script: *"We call this 'solving by elimination'. The computer does the same thing when it answers your search — it eliminates the pages you don't want and gives you the ones that are left."*
- **Apply (30 min):** Students solve SH Ch 6 vacation questions. Pairs check each other's grid. Teacher walks the room.
- **Reflect (5 min):** *"What did you cross out first? Why?"*

**Offline fallback:** already offline.
**Bilingual delivery note:** day names chalked in all three languages. Telugu: ఆదివారం, సోమవారం…; Urdu: اتوار, پیر…; English: Sunday, Monday…
**Parent-facing output:** no.
**Misconception map:**
  1. "I'll solve it in my head." Catch: *"The grid is faster. Draw it."*

### 7.1 Rubric (4×4)

| | Novice (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Mastery (4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | Attempts, needs teacher step-by-step | Completes with 1-2 teacher prompts | Completes independently, correct result | Completes + adds a variation on their own |
| **Ethics** | Needs reminder to be honest / kind / inclusive | Reminds themselves after prompting | Acts honest/kind/inclusive without prompting | Catches a peer and helps them act well |
| **Communication** | Struggles to explain | Explains in 1 language only, hesitant | Explains in 2 languages, clear | Explains + answers an unexpected question |
| **Effort** | Gives up quickly | Attempts, loses focus | Sustained attempt, finishes | Finishes + helps a peer |

- **Term 1:** Week 1 worksheet · Week 2 three-column list · Week 3 labelled diagram · Week 4 Caesar wheel photo + encoded card photo · Weeks 5-10 worksheets · Week 11 pixel grid · Week 12 festival card photo

Grade 3 has no primary written exam. Teachers may give a short pen-and-paper comprehension at mid-year (Week 24) and year-end (Week 36) if the school demands it — these are informal, 20 minutes each, and do not drive promotion decisions.

## 8. Teacher delivery notes

### 8.1 Trainer script

Every CT+AI Lab teacher must complete:
1. A 2-day pre-term workshop (16 hours), aligned to CBSE Centre of Excellence DLDs (`ai/cbse/teacher-training-coe/CBSE_COE_DETAILS.html`) and CBSE Training Notification TRG-02/2026 (`ai/cbse/circulars-and-notifications/TRG-02-2026.pdf` from Phase 1 archive)
2. A 1-day mid-year refresh (Week 18 weekend)
3. Monthly 1-hour online check-ins

### 8.2 Prep checklist per session

Every session needs the teacher to arrive 10 minutes early with: printables sorted into 40 piles · any hardware (tray, wheels, tablets, robots) laid out at the back of the room · board pre-chalked with the bilingual glossary · the Student Handbook open at the correct page · the week's rubric sheet ready.

### 8.3 Misconception map

Collated across all 36 sessions — the two most common year-long errors are:
- **"The computer / AI is thinking."** Correct framing: *"The computer / AI is matching patterns that people taught it. When the pattern is new, it fails."*
- **"If it's in English, it's better."** Correct framing: *"English works first because the people who built it heard more English. That is a problem we spend our whole life fixing."*

### 8.4 Equipment logistics for the year

- One parts tray (Week 3) — stored in a locked cupboard, replaced parts as they wear
- 40 Caesar wheels (Week 4) — each student keeps theirs; teacher has 5 spare
- 10 tablets with ScratchJr (Weeks 20-22) — charged Friday evenings, locked away
- 5 line-follower robots (Week 23) — batteries kept charged; teacher checks Thursday
- Reusable stick bundles, bean bowls, measuring cups (various weeks) — in a labelled shared tray
- Printables master pack of 500 pages per term — ordered from local print shop one week ahead (cost ≈ ₹1,000/term × 3 terms = ₹3,000/year, divided across 40 students = ₹75/student/year marginal)
