# 07 — AI careers for school students

**v1 · 2026-11-21 · branch `research/ai-5-to-10-v1`**

Career content is the part of this programme most likely to be dishonest, because it is the part parents care about most and the part nobody can check for ten years. This file is written to be defensible in 2036.

**Evidence grades used throughout:** **[GOV]** government release or official announcement · **[NEWS]** credible journalism · **[ANNOUNCED]** a commitment, not delivered capacity · **[JUDGEMENT]** our reading, labelled as ours.

---

## 1. The frame: what we tell a 13-year-old, and what we refuse to say

**What we refuse to say.** That AI is a guaranteed career. That their chosen stream decides their life. That a specific job title will exist when they are 25. That learning Python now means a salary later. Every one of those is a sentence a competitor's brochure contains and none of them survives contact with the actual labour market.

**What we say instead, and it is more useful.** The work that will exist is work where someone understands a real domain **and** can judge whether a machine's output about that domain is right. That is the same skill the whole programme has been building — `05-evaluations.md` is, read sideways, a careers document.

**[JUDGEMENT]** A frank note that belongs in a document and not in a parent meeting: the current AI and software labour market in India shows signs of being priced ahead of itself. Announced investment is not delivered capacity, and announced headcount is not hiring. We should teach the capability and let the market be whatever it turns out to be, rather than tying the programme's credibility to a boom.

## 2. What AI work actually is

The single most useful correction to make to a school student, and most adults: **the large majority of paid AI work is not building models.**

| Role family | What the day actually looks like | Entry route | How much maths |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data work** — data engineer, data analyst, annotation lead | Getting messy data into a usable shape. Cleaning, joining, checking, labelling. This is where most of the hours in the industry are | Diploma or degree + demonstrable projects | Moderate. Statistics matters more than calculus |
| **AI-adjacent software** — backend, platform, MLOps | Building the things models live inside. Deployment, monitoring, cost, uptime | CS degree or strong self-taught portfolio | Moderate |
| **Domain expert who can judge AI** — doctor, agronomist, teacher, lawyer, accountant, mechanic | Their own profession, plus the judgement to know when the AI tool is wrong. **Fastest-growing category and least talked about** | The domain qualification, first | Varies by domain |
| **Infrastructure and facilities** — data-centre technician, electrical, cooling, networks | Keeping the physical building running. Power, liquid cooling, racks, security | **ITI or diploma. No degree required** | Low |
| **Model building** — ML engineer, research scientist | Actually training and designing models | Usually a master's or PhD | **High.** Linear algebra, probability, calculus |
| **Making the data honest** — testing, red-teaming, audit, policy | Finding where a system fails and who it fails for | Mixed; often non-technical entry | Low to moderate |

**The two rows a Telangana student should look at hardest** are the domain-expert row and the infrastructure row. The first because it is the largest and the least contested. The second because **it does not require a degree**, it is physically located in Telangana, and nobody is telling school students about it.

**The row every student thinks they want** is the last-but-one, and it is the smallest, the most credentialed and the most geographically concentrated. We should not discourage it — some of our students will be good at it — but presenting it as *the* AI career is the central dishonesty of AI career talks.

## 3. What is actually in Telangana

Specific, dated, and graded. These are the things a Siddipet parent can be told about without exaggeration.

| What | Detail | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **TCS HyperVault AI data centre** | 264 acres at Future City near Hyderabad, designed for up to **1 GW**, investment up to **₹70,000 crore**, announced 5 Sep 2026. Telangana pegs roughly **7,000 direct and indirect roles** | **[ANNOUNCED]** — built in phases. Not yet jobs |
| **NTT DATA + Neysa Networks cluster** | **₹10,500 crore**, 400 MW, ~25,000 GPUs, Hyderabad, MoU Apr 2025 | **[ANNOUNCED]** |
| **TASK + AWS skilling agreement** | Signed **2 Nov 2026**. Free AI and cloud training for **1 lakh students across 643 TASK-affiliated colleges**, at no cost to the state, the colleges or the students. Includes data engineering. TASK to run job fairs for those who complete it | **[GOV]** — **the single most concrete item in this table for our students** |
| **Green Skills & Applied AI Centre of Excellence** | Launched 23 Oct 2026 at SAS iTower, with Microsoft, the 1M1B Foundation and MeitY Startup Hub. Trains science and technology **graduates** in applied AI, green pharma, solar, EV, wind. 100 job offers on launch day | **[NEWS]**, consistent across three outlets |
| **Telangana AI Mission (T-AIM)**, AI City Hyderabad | State AI mission with NASSCOM; dedicated AI hub | **[GOV]**, longer-standing |
| **Microsoft Hyderabad AI region** | AI-ready capacity plus a Data Center Academy pipeline | **[ANNOUNCED]** |
| **Agriculture** | Saagu Baagu, the chilli-farmer AI advisory programme — see `06-story-bank.md` entry 1 | **[NEWS]** with the project's own reported figures flagged as such |

**Why the TASK + AWS line is the one to put in front of a parent.** It is free, it is government-run, it covers 643 colleges in their own state, it names data engineering specifically, and it ends in job fairs. A student currently in our Grade 10 enters college in roughly 2029 — comfortably inside this pipeline's life. **It is the only item in the table that is a pathway rather than a press release.** Everything else is a building.

**The honest caveat on the data centres.** A gigawatt campus employs a large number of people in construction and a much smaller number permanently, and the permanent roles skew to facilities, power, cooling, networking and security rather than to machine learning. That is good news for the ITI and diploma route and should be said that way, not dressed up.

## 4. The pathway out of Class 10

What a Grade 10 student actually chooses, and what each choice keeps open.

| After Class 10 | What it is | Keeps open | Closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MPC** (Maths, Physics, Chemistry) | The engineering route | B.Tech incl. CSE/AI, the model-building path, data engineering | Medicine |
| **BiPC** | The life-sciences route | Medicine, pharma, biotech — and **AI-in-healthcare**, which is a real and growing domain-expert path in Hyderabad's pharma cluster | Engineering |
| **MEC / CEC** | Commerce and economics | Data analysis, business analytics, finance, actuarial. **Underrated for AI work** — statistics is the relevant maths and commerce students get it | B.Tech |
| **Polytechnic diploma** (after Class 10) | Three-year diploma, then lateral entry to B.Tech or direct employment | Data-centre technician, networks, electrical, instrumentation. **Fastest route to a paid AI-adjacent job** | Nothing permanently — lateral entry exists |
| **ITI** | Trade certification | Electrical, electronics, networking — the physical layer of every data centre named in §3 | Degree routes without later bridging |
| **Code 417 at Class 9-10** | The CBSE AI skill subject, if the school offers it and the student picked it | Nothing in particular — it is not a prerequisite for anything | Nothing |

**Two things to say plainly to a Grade 10 and their parents.**

First, **417 is not a gatekeeper.** It is a good subject and a board-examined one, and a student who takes it arrives in Class 11 with real Python and a real portfolio. But no engineering, polytechnic or college route requires it. A student who did not take it has lost nothing structural. Saying otherwise, to sell our Track A, would be a lie we would get caught in.

Second, **MPC is not the only AI route and polytechnic is not a failure route.** The diploma path leads to the physically-located, degree-free, in-Telangana jobs in §3. For a Siddipet family weighing cost and distance against a four-year degree, that is a real option and almost nobody tells them so.

## 5. Career awareness, grade by grade

Mapped to the syllabus in `01` so it rides inside existing units rather than needing its own slot. Total cost: roughly one session per grade per year plus the Grade 7 project.

| Grade | What they learn about work | Where it sits | Artefact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | That people are paid to make the machines they use. Named, visible jobs only — not categories | Unit 1, in the "AI or Not?" audit: who maintains each thing they listed? | A line on the wall chart naming who looks after each item |
| **6** | That data is somebody's job. The person who collected the rainfall numbers exists | Unit 3 / the weather project — whose job was it to record this? | One sentence in the project poster naming the data's source institution |
| **7** | **The real thing: one verified local AI deployment, and what it replaced** | **Unit 8 is already this project** — the industry investigation | 5-slide deck + an interview if a person was reachable |
| **8** | That AI work includes finding what is wrong with AI. Testing and audit as jobs | Unit 8, the misinformation hunt: who does this for a living? | A paragraph in the hunt log on who gets paid to do this |
| **9** | The role map in §2, honestly, including which rows are small. First look at Class 11 options | A single session inside Track A A6 / Track B B5 | A one-page "three routes I could take" sheet, written by the student |
| **10** | The Class 11 decision, with §4 on the table. Portfolio as evidence | Track A A7 / Track B B5 | **Portfolio of 5+ artefacts + a one-page Class 11 plan with the reason written down** |

**The design principle:** no careers content before Grade 7 that is not a specific named person doing a specific named job. Category talk ("data scientists are in demand") at Grade 5 is noise. A Grade 7 who has interviewed one person at one poultry farm using one camera system knows more about AI work than a Grade 10 who has read a list of job titles.

## 6. For the parent meeting

The four questions that actually get asked, with the answer we give.

**"Will this get my child a job?"**
No programme can promise that, and we are not going to. What it does is build two things that transfer: the ability to work with data, and the judgement to tell when a machine's output is wrong. Those matter in almost every career now, including ones with nothing to do with technology. If your child ends up a doctor or an accountant, the second one still matters.

**"Should my child take AI as a subject in Class 9?"**
Only if they are interested. It is a good subject with a board exam and a real portfolio at the end. But no college, diploma or engineering route requires it. Do not let anyone tell you your child falls behind without it.

**"My child wants to work in AI. What stream?"**
Depends which AI work. Building models needs MPC and then heavy maths, and it is the smallest of the categories. Data analysis is at least as open from commerce. AI in medicine and pharma runs through BiPC. And the data centres being built in Telangana right now will need technicians, electricians and network people — a polytechnic diploma is a genuine route to those and a shorter one.

**"Is this a bubble?"**
Possibly in parts. A lot of what is announced is investment, not jobs yet. That is one reason we teach judgement and data skills rather than one tool or one job title — those keep their value whichever way the market goes.
